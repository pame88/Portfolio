[Dragon Admin Center documentation on Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/industry/healthcare/dragon-admin-center/)

The Dragon Admin Center (DAC) is the admin center supporting Microsoft's
clinical AI solutions, namely Dragon Copilot. In DAC, admins can control their
clinicians' experience within Dragon Copilot through permission management
based on their organization's unique needs and configuration. They can
provision products for their end users and manage what they have access to
within those products.

### Problem statement / How might we

- Allow management of multiple environments within DAC, each with its own
  content and configuration.
- Allow flexibility in configurations to account for varying organization
  sizes and structures.
- Support intuitive, easy navigation while avoiding repetition in content
  through cross-linking.
- Make it easy to switch views between users and organizational units.

### Goal

Lay the foundation for Microsoft's Health and Life Sciences (HLS) ecosystem by
providing a critical support platform for Dragon Copilot and all future
Microsoft HLS products.

### My contributions

- Creating UX specifications for handoff to developers.
- Creating a design system including a component toolkit and UX patterns.
- Creating accessibility specifications and getting them approved.
- Reviewing implementation for consistency with specifications.

    <SectionHeading

      as="h3"

    One simple but effective solution that I conceived was the environment
    picker. DAC allows multiple environments to be created, each containing a
    unique organization configuration and content. Customer admins need to
    easily switch between environments to make edits or compare configurations.

    **How might we:** Enable a way to easily switch between environments at any
    level of the application.

    **Customer need:**

    - Quickly compare configurations between environments.
    - Quickly compare content between environments.

    #### The solution

  </div>
  <ImageCarousel
    id="environment-solution"
    label="Environment picker solution"
    slides={environmentSlides}
  />
  <div class="flow">

    This solution facilitates easy comparison between environments, allowing the
    admin to navigate to the content they want to see and switch environments in
    that context, rather than switching somewhere else and navigating back to
    the content. It works across different content types, such as list pages and
    details pages for environments or users. It's dynamic too: if a detail page
    can't be replicated in a different environment, it reverts to the common
    root page.

  </div>

<Section id="remove-user-data">
  <div class="flow">

    <SectionHeading
      id="feature-highlight-remove-user-data"
      title="Remove user data"
      label="Feature 03"
      as="h3"
    />

    An especially challenging feature to design was removing user data. To
    comply with privacy requirements, we needed to allow removal of user data
    from the admin center. The challenge was that we did not yet have a
    directory of users or a user details page.

    **How might we:** Enable removal of user data from the admin center without
    a user directory.

    **Requirements:**

    - Allow bulk removal.
    - Provide clear messaging when each removal succeeds or fails without
      crowding the UI.
    - Consider the asynchronous removal process: allow admins to run removals
      in the background, but display appropriate messaging when needed.

    #### The solution

  </div>
  <ImageCarousel
    id="removal-solution"
    label="Remove user data solution"
    slides={removalSlides}
  />
  <div class="flow">

    By combining a multi-user selector and a table within a drawer, I enabled
    bulk removal and bulk status updates without crowding the main UI with
    individual toast messages. The user can check removal statuses at any time,
    but also knows without checking whether removal is still in progress
    through a message bar, or whether an operation has failed through a toast.
    This lets the admin continue performing tasks while removals occur without
    worrying about forgetting or missing an error.

  </div>
</Section>

    Another feature I designed that required an innovative, custom solution was
    adding EHR (electronic health record) IDs.

    **How might we:** Enable addition of new content to existing objects in bulk,
    allowing a custom selection of objects to add content to.

    **Customer need:**

    - Add EHR IDs to existing users in bulk.
    - Modify the list before submitting: delete, add more, or edit.

    #### The solution

    **New pattern:** Add new content to existing objects in bulk.

  </div>

    <SectionHeading
      id="leveraging-microsoft-admin-design-system-mads"
      title="Leveraging Microsoft Admin Design System (MADS)"
      label="Process 02"
      as="h3"
      tone="violet"
    />

    The [Microsoft Admin Design System](https://admincontrolsdemoapps.z22.web.core.windows.net/storybook/latest/Storybook/?path=/docs/about--docs)
    is a design system developed for teams within Microsoft building admin
    centers. Our UX team was interested in how we might leverage MADS, so we met
    with its primary contact, Aaron Woo.

    Over several meetings, we determined which components we could use and how
    to follow their guidance. Although MADS was built on Fluent v8 rather than
    v9, which we designed with, we still used their left navigation component
    and took direct inspiration for our layout templates and design patterns.

    I created our own design system as the foundation for DAC.

    #### UX specifications

    The heart of the work in designing the Admin Center is the UX
    specifications. They are critical to developer handoff: workflows are laid
    out, components are used in context, and developer notes inform the
    implementation.

    Workflows are separated by section, and each screen has a title and
    annotations describing dependencies, actions, and more.

  </div>
  <ResponsiveImage {...processImages.uxWorkflows} />
  <ResponsiveImage {...processImages.uxAnnotations} />
  <div class="flow">

    #### Accessibility specifications

    Another critical aspect of preparing the design for general availability
    was meeting Microsoft's accessibility standards. This multifaceted process
    involved:

    - Getting familiar with Microsoft's accessibility standards for design and
      documentation.
    - Creating specifications detailing the accessibility of our screens and
      components.
    - Getting these specifications approved by the Microsoft Cloud + AI
      Accessibility team.
    - Handing specifications off to developers for implementation.

  </div>
  <ResponsiveImage {...processImages.accessibilitySpecification} />
  <ResponsiveImage {...processImages.accessibilityAnnotations} />

    #### UX reviews

    Once the designs were implemented, it was also my responsibility to review:

    - **Functionality:** Does everything work?
    - **Adherence to spec:** Does everything work and look as intended?
    - **Accessibility:** Is all the accessibility correct?

    After GA of DAC, there was product direction to gauge customer impressions
    of the platform. We had several private-preview customers with early
    access, including Braden Health, who we could interface with. I led the
    effort to gather feedback on the existing UX and understand their
    expectations and ideal improvements.

    After an initial survey, we met to dissect the responses, followed by a
    series of deep dives into specific features and enhancement requests.
    Those requests were triaged as tickets in our ADO backlog, helping
    prioritize post-GA features and informing our design approach.
